# GDV-Stellungnahme zu EU-Spar- und Anlagekonten

# Kurzfassung

Der GDV begrüßt das Ziel der EU, privates Kapital für Europas Zukunft zu mobilisieren. Damit das gelingt, müssen vor allem langfristige Investitionen gefördert werden – etwa in Rente, Innovation, Infrastruktur und Transformation. Versicherungsanlageprodukte sind für dieses Ziel prädestiniert und ein unverzichtbarer Teil der Lösung. (mehr)

To reflect the full range of effective solutions, including IBIPs, the scope of the Commission recommendation should be neutral with respect to eligible providers or explicitly include insurance products.

We explicitly support European initiatives on supplementary pensions and the Finance Europe label. While requiring public funds, these initiatives pursue clear societal objectives and address long-term structural challenges such as the pension gap. It is essential to ensure that any additional use of fiscal resources for "Savings and Investment Accounts' (SIAs) is similarly targeted and justified. The Commission recommendation needs to recognise this fact.

The Commission emphasises that SIAs should serve both short-term and longterm needs of retail investors. They should also be easily portable. However, to achieve the goal of channelling private capital into the economy, more investment in long-term assets such as private equity, venture capital and other alternative investments is urgently needed. A characteristic feature of these investments is that they are not suitable for short-term liquidity needs. If SIAs are to support the goals of the Savings and Investment Union, they must be clearly aligned with longterm investment. This includes linking SIAs to the proposed 'Finance Europe' label.

Furthermore, a rigid portability requirement risks excluding product types that are otherwise well-suited for SIAs and long-term and illiquid capital formation. Therefore,  functional  portability  such  as  value  transfer  or  reinvestment  mechanisms should be implemented instead. Otherwise, public resources risk being misallocated, undermining the overall goals of the SIU.

## 2.  Include IBIPs explicitly in the scope

The Commission's consultation paper highlights the importance of enabling retail investors  to  access  'a  broad  set  of  investment  opportunities,  such  as  equities, bonds, and investment funds, as well as other financial products'. IBIPs clearly fall within this category and have demonstrated high uptake and strong long-term saving effects.

Insurance products are a cornerstone of long-term investment and retirement savings and must be recognised in well-functioning regimes. With their long savings and decumulation phases, IBIPs are ideal to serve the goals of the SIU. Unlike ELTIFs,  IBIPs  also  enable  small  investors  with  low  monthly  savings  rates  to participate  in  alternative  investments  such  as  infrastructure,  thereby  supporting productive investment in the European economy. These solutions combine longterm capital commitment for the economy with very attractive return opportunities for small investors.  For this reason, the Finance Europe label rightly includes IBIPs in the scope.

Including them explicitly in the scope of a future SIA Recommendation - and not only within an account, which contradicts their operational logic - would ensure that a large and well-established segment of the retail savings infrastructure is fully reflected. The term 'account' as currently proposed by the Commission risks implying a banking-only product architecture and would lead to an unlevel playing field. To maintain competitive neutrality and to ensure alignment with the 'Finance Europe' label, more inclusive terminology should be adopted - such as 'wrapper', 'product', or 'solution'.

SIA-type concepts are already embedded in several Member States' systems. In countries where such regimes exist, insurance-based products (försäkring) necessarily  fall  within the  same regulatory and fiscal scope. For example, in Sweden endowment insurance is treated the same way as the ISK. The EC should explicitly acknowledge this in the recommendation.

## 3.  Fiscal incentives must follow public value

We support the Commission's objectives of using SIAs to facilitate investment in the European economy and enable small investors to make better investments. These objectives can justify the use of (scarce) public funds, provided they serve clear overarching public policy goals.

This is not the case, for example, with short-term investments. While short-term equity savings may be attractive for individual financial goals or general wealth accumulation, they do not generate added value for the broader economy or society. Such savings typically flow into shares of large corporations that do not face financing  constraints.  The  amounts  invested neither  stimulate  additional investment within the EU nor provide a reliable contribution to securing living standards in old age.

While the Commission leaves the design of tax incentives to the Member States, we caution against the use of public funds - such as preferential tax treatment for short-term, general-purpose investment accounts that lack a clear societal objective. Where tax incentives are considered, they should be targeted toward longterm savings and investment objectives that align with EU priorities, such as retirement security, infrastructure, or the green and digital transitions.

## 4.  Establish a Link between SIAs and the 'Finance Europe' Label

The German insurance industry would welcome linking the SIA concept to the proposed 'Finance Europe' label. This would ensure that only products meeting high standards of long-term orientation, transparency, consumer protection, and sustainability benefit from fiscal or regulatory preferences - thereby reinforcing trust and uptake. It would also support a more coherent and navigable European investment landscape, which is already becoming increasingly complex with the introduction of the PEPP and the ongoing IORP review.

In  our  view,  SIAs should incorporate a minimum holding period to encourage a long-term investment mindset and enable meaningful exposure to illiquid assets, such as infrastructure and private markets. Portability should not undermine longterm investment strategies. An easy exit option risks disincentivising the inclusion of assets that are critical to the EU's strategic goals such as innovation, infrastructure, clean and digital transitions, defence and other key priorities of the EU.
