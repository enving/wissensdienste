# GDV-Stellungnahme zur EU-Sondierung zu ergänzender Altersvorsorge

# Kurzfassung

Der GDV begrüßt das Ziel der EU, die ergänzende Altersvorsorge zu stärken für die Zukunft der EU-Bürger. Dafür müssen insbesondere langfristige Investitionen gefördert werden – um die Wettbewerbsfähigkeit der Wirtschaft nachhaltig zu sichern. Versicherungsanlageprodukte sind für dieses Ziel prädestiniert und ein unverzichtbarer Teil der Lösung. (mehr)

on the Call for Evidence for an Initiative: Supplementary pensions - review of the regulatory framework and other measures to strengthen the sector

The German insurers welcome the European Commission's initiative to strengthen the supplementary pensions sector as a key pillar of the European Savings and Investments Union (SIU). The sustainability of pension systems and the adequacy of retirement income are increasingly under pressure due to demographic trends. At the same time, the EU urgently needs to mobilise more long-term capital to finance its competitiveness, productivity and digital and sustainable transformation of economy.

We strongly support the political focus on pensions and long-term savings as expressed in President von der Leyen's mission letter to Commissioner Albuquerque and in the Commission's SIU Communication of March 2025. We also welcome the strategic perspectives presented in the Draghi and Letta reports. They rightly underline the central role that pension savings can play in funding the real economy

## Annex:

## 1.  Review of the IORP Directive

The current IORP II Directive sets an appropriate, proportionate and risk-based supervisory framework that ensures transparency and member protection. It rightly follows a minimum harmonisation approach, allowing national authorities to reflect local labour, tax and social law conditions. Further standardisation would not enhance fairness but risk undermining effective national solutions. Supervisory convergence does not require identical methods but shared objectives. There is no empirical evidence of regulatory arbitrage.

The existing IORP II regulation has been implemented nationally and is now being applied by companies and NCAs. The directive should be given time to prove itself. Further  detailed  regulation  would  contradict  the  idea  of  minimal  harmonisation, cause enormous difficulties and effort for IORPs and contradict the efforts to reduce bureaucracy. In our opinion existing heterogenous structures in each member state should always be taken into account. In Germany, for example, there are currently very few defined contribution plans (DC). Against this background, requirements for DC solutions with a standardised EU-wide pension product (i.e., PEPP in occupational pension schemes) would not be appropriate. Furthermore, an already existing reasonable cost reporting system to the NCA should not be counteracted by new requirements with a not purposeful level of detail or completely different logic. This might contradict the objectives regarding efficiency, affordability and good value for money. Therefore, we believe that the national characteristics can best be taken into account by the NCA. NCAs like BaFin are well equipped to enforce the prudent person principle under Article 19. Proportionality remains a core principle and must guide any future adjustments to the framework.

## 2.  PEPP Review

The low uptake of the PEPP results from its overly complex and prescriptive design.  Mandatory  sub-accounts,  PEPP-specific  documents,  rigid  risk  mitigation rules, and the 1% cost cap create unnecessary burdens and discourage providers. PEPP should be flexible enough to fit the national tax regimes for pensions. For example, the Basic PEPP should allow product diversity, not impose lifecycle strategies. The cost cap also contradicts the objectives of the SIU. The channelling of savings  into  the  European  economy  can  only  succeed  with  alternative  assets, which are not very cheap. Products with a strict cost focus cannot invest in private equity, private debt or venture capital. We support transforming PEPP into a label to reflect local saver needs and market realities more effectively.

Finally, the PEPP should first be allowed to establish itself as a functioning private pension product before additional complexity is introduced by extending it to occupational pensions.

## 3.  Auto-enrolment

We support an auto-enrolment that is voluntary for employers and opt-out-based with flexible implementation at national level. The default product should be chosen by employers in cooperation with providers, not imposed centrally. Collective bargaining must not be mandatory, as this would create barriers for employers without access to social partners and limit coverage. A centralised state or standard product would hinder competition and innovation. Providers must compete openly, ensuring product diversity.

## 4.  Pension Tracking

Germany launched its Digital Pension Overview on 1 January 2025, covering all three pillars. All insurers are connected, but uptake by citizens remains low due to limited access via the online ID card. Broader use requires alternative, user-friendly authentication options and stronger public awareness. A successful tracking system must be simple, digital, and intuitive, while ensuring data security and objectivity.  It  should  include all pension pillars and deliver projections to help identify pension gaps. Implementation must be efficient to lower the burden for providers. Otherwise cost barriers can hamper broad participation.
