## Introduction

German Insurers strongly agree with the need to simplify the current SFDR to regain its purpose as a customer disclosure tool. We are not convinced that establishing a categorisation system must be the first step for this purpose. However, if the European Commission is committed to such a system, the introduction of a new categorisation system should be embedded also in an updated IDD framework for sustainability preferences. We support both the Platform on Sustainable Finance (PSF) and the German 'Sustainable Finance Beirat' approaches for a new categorisation regime. Below you find our comments on these suggestions and further ideas for simplifications.

## Key Issues for German Insurers in upcoming SFDR Review

## 1. Insurance specific issues

Insurers have a unique business model governed by sector specific prudential and conduct legislation (i.e. Solvency II, IDD etc.). Moreover, Insurance Based Investment Products (IBIPs) are unique products that often combine direct investment with secure investments through a 'general account' of the insurer as well as with additional insurance cover. As a result, the following points need to be considered in any upcoming SFDR Review:

- Consider the general account: Life insurers invest the premiums in a single large pot, the so-called general account. There are many regulatory requirements for this general account. The central principles are asset mix, diversification, safety, and returns. This prevents a short-term and complete shift into pure Sustainable investments. This must therefore be considered both when defining the categories themselves and when setting thresholds, to ensure  that  products  with  (partial)  investment  in  the  general  account  can achieve a Sustainable category. As such, it is essential that the wide range of investment instruments that such portfolios are composed of (e.g. including government bonds) is covered by the metrics upon which the potential categorization system would be built.
- Consider Multi-Option Products (MOPs): Life insurers also offer their customers the option of investing in mutual funds. Typically, there is a large selection of fixed income, government bonds, real estate and equity funds. A wide range is also usually offered within these fund classes, with the result that the overall portfolio can often contain a three-digit number of funds. It should be ensured that this large selection would not have to be extremely restricted following the introduction of a Sustainability categorization system. This could be achieved, for example, by using a 'mixed' category, or the 'ESG Collection' category in the PSF proposal.
- A categorization system should serve as the only regulatory classification system, so that a customer shouldn't have to comprehend differences (and

potential incoherences) between how a fund is called and how it is categorized (on that, ESMA categories should be immersed with the categorization system).

## 2. General Principles for simplification

No matter which approach for the review is chosen we believe the following principles are key to achieve a meaningful simplification of the SFDR:

- Radically simplify product specific information requirements: As with the omnibus initiative, the SFDR review should be used for comprehensive simplification. The product specific information required under the SFDR (Articles 7 to 11 SFDR) is much too extensive and too complex for retail investors. As a result, retail investors ignore the information or may even be discouraged  from  considering  sustainability-related  aspects  of  their  investments. This is especially important in the case of the mandatory templates prescribed under the SFDR-RTS for pre-contractual and annual information on Article 8 and 9 products. Information, which must be provided for all customers should be very simple in order to attract the retail investors' attention and provide key facts. This would also simplify the advisory process for intermediaries.  More  detailed  -  yet  not  excessively  detailed  -  information should be provided online in an easily accessible format for those customers who wish to know more. It is important that information requirements are consumer tested and - moreover - that the results of the tests are considered.
- Furthermore, experience (web site click rates) shows that the information required by Articles 3, 4 and 5 SFDR were visited only by extremely few viewers. Since company-related information will be provided under the CSRD, such information should not be doubled under the SFDR.
- Use the SFDR Review to also fix the dysfunctional MIFID/IDD sustainability preferences system: Currently the legal framework on the sustainability preferences is too complex and too rigid. The review should focus on improving comprehensibility and handling for the benefit of consumers and intermediaries. Simplification of the process and of the legal framework is essential. Alignment with the future SFDR is an important first step. The categorization system should be the basis for the integration of sustainability preferences  as  part  of  the  suitability  assessment.  However,  simplification needs to go further. Currently EIOPA guidance (BOS-22-391) imposes extensive requirements on how insurers must design the process, including extremely  detailed  provisions  on the  sequence  of questions. These  requirements  have  proven  to  be  discouraging  to  consumers  and  intermediaries alike.  New  MiFID  II/IDD  regulation  will  need  to  ensure  that  advisory  processes benefit from simplifications of the legal framework and that no counterproductive  complexity  is  added  at  Level  3.  Example:  The  current

framework requires insurers to first establish the suitability of the product in financial  terms  (step  one)  and  only  then  enquire  the  sustainability  preferences of the customer (step two). In practice this artificial separation of the financial and the sustainability-related elements of the suitability assessment leads to frequent mismatches and frustration. To avoid this, the customer should be allowed to view the sustainability category during the advisory process to enable him to choose the overall package.

- The definition of 'Sustainable Investment' (SI) in accordance with Article 2 (17) SFDR should be clarified further, i.e. a common methodology for the calculation of SI should be determined (including on how sovereigns fall into this definition).
- The SFDR review and Principle Adverse Impact (PAI) disclosure should be brought in consistency with the (improved) DNSH principle.
- Remove  company-related  CSRD  information  from  the  SFDR  information: We support replacing entity level reporting under SFDR by aggregated Group level reporting under CSRD. Experience (click rates) shows that the information required by Articles 3, 4 and 5 SFDR was visited only by extremely few viewers. Since company-related information will be provided under the CSRD, such information should not be doubled under the SFDR. Upon (group aggregate) PAI disclosure under CSRD, the Art. 4 SFDR requirement for entity level disclosure should be deemed as complied with.
- Ensure consistency with other legislation: Interdependencies between SFDR and CSRD concerning data availability should be considered, i.e. any intended changes to the SFDR PAIs need to be preceded by a change in the ESRS to avoid inconsistent or missing data. Thus, regarding the review of sector-agnostic ESRS, the datapoints in cross cutting standards and topical standards that derive from other EU legislation (ESRS 2 Appendix B) should be directly reflected in CSRD reporting requirements. These include, in particular, the 16 Principle Adverse Impact (PAI) indicators required for SFDR disclosure. In addition, the number of mandatory PAIs should be reduced. Also, the VSME standard should include these datapoints as sector agnostic indicators.
- Regarding transition finance concepts, the interlinkages with CSRD (disclosure of transition targets) and CSDDD (requirement to implement transition targets) should be considered.
- The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, as amended by the recent EmpCo Directive stipulates strict requirements for environmental claims. For example, generic environmental claims are forbidden under the Directive, unless they are backed by a sustainability label or similar. It is important to clarify that any categories determined by the SFDR can be included in the name and description of the respective product in order to facilitate identification by consumers.

## 3. Comments on the Proposal by the Platform for Sustainable Finance

We welcome that the PSF proposal seeks to utilise existing EU regulation as far as possible in particular that t is built on the Sustainable Finance Framework, using tools such as the EU Taxonomy, EU Climate Benchmarks, EU Green Bond Standards, Principal Adverse Impact indicators. More concretely:

- Insurers  support  the  introduction  of  a  'transition  category'  qualified through  criteria  such  as exclusions  strategy;  pre-defined,  measurable, positive Environmental-, Social-, Governance-related outcome; credible transition  plan.  Climate  transition  targets  should  include  carbon  removal  and evaluation  of  transition  pathways  of  sovereigns  by  using  available  frameworks  like  Assessing  Sovereign  Climate-related  Opportunities  and  Risks (ASCOR).
- Special features of insurance and pension products and investments in the general account are recognised: Solvency II restrictions must be strictly adhered to by insurance companies. These restrictions are not explicitly addressed by the PSF, but it is mentioned several times that 'hedging and liquidity instruments' can be excluded - so such a part of the respective product or a part underlying different legal restrictions does not have to comply with any sustainability requirements. This is probably aimed more at funds, but should in principle be applied to Insurers' 'general account'
- MOPs (and Fund of Funds) are partly considered but need more explicit rules: Despite the lack of a 'mixed' category - the ESG Collection category by the PSF should, however, also be explicitly open to MOPs. It should be possible to mix funds from the transition and sustainable category.
- Any considerations around the introduction of thresholds for certain categories should consider market realities, in particular, insurance products currently available on the market and their respective minimum shares.
