## Introduction

The German insurance industry welcomes and fully supports EFRAG's ongoing efforts to streamline the ESRS and to develop reporting requirements that are both effective and workable for undertakings of all sizes and sectors. Insights from the first round of CSRD reporting-as well as an initial review of published sustainability reports-have clearly shown that the current requirements are often overly complex and insufficiently aligned with real-world business practices. Moreover, not all data points are equally relevant for investors, credit institutions, or insurers. The growing complexity and volume of the requirements risk undermining the quality, comparability, and usefulness of reported information-thus weakening the foundation for informed investment decisions.

Against this background, we seek to make a constructive contribution to the revision of ESRS Set 1 by putting forward a set of cross-cutting proposals. These aim to  streamline  the standards while preserving their ambition and sector-agnostic applicability. Addressing overarching principles-rather than focusing on individual datapoints-offers the greatest potential to improve clarity, proportionality, and usability across sectors. At the same time, we stress the importance of retaining key elements of the CSRD framework, particularly the principle of double materiality, which remains essential for meaningful and comparable sustainability reporting.

The following proposals reflect our priorities for a more targeted, workable, and effective ESRS.

## GDV Proposals for the revision of ESRS Set 1

## Proposal 1: Introduction of a General Clause for 'True and Fair View' and Strengthening of the Management Approach in Sustainability Reporting

To give a true and fair view of an undertaking's sustainability reporting under the CSRD, a general clause should be introduced - similar to the one established for financial reporting under Article 4 (3) and (4) of the EU Accounting Directive. 'True and Fair View' is an overarching principle meaning it can justify deviations from specific requirements if necessary to achieve a clearer picture.

While  the  double  materiality  principle  is  explicitly  mentioned  in  Article  19a,  the ESRS are currently perceived as imposing a rather rigid structure on sustainability reporting. Introducing a General Clause for 'True and Fair View' could enhance the  flexibility  and  ensure  that  CSRD  reports  are  not  just  accurate  according  to ESRS but also provide a reliable and understandable representation of a company's sustainability situation. The 'True and Fair View' principle has long been a cornerstone of financial reporting. There is no clear reason to leave out a General Clause from sustainability reporting.

We  propose  to  amend  ESRS  1,  Chapter  2 Qualitative  characteristics  of  information , by adding the following paragraph:

'The CSRD report at large shall provide a true and fair view of the undertaking's impacts on sustainability matters, and of how sustainability matters affect the undertaking's development, performance and position.

Where the application of this paragraph alone is not sufficient to provide a true and fair view, additional disclosures shall be provided.'

This principle should be complemented by a management approach, allowing disclosures to follow the logic of how the business is actually managed. Such alignment supports a reporting logic based on the actual substance of business operations rather than formalistic structures. It gives both undertakings and their auditors greater interpretive flexibility, enabling a more focused and judgment-based application of the reporting requirements.

## Proposal 2: Strengthening materiality of information, ensuring relevance and information value for users of the reports

The general aim is to strengthen the concept of information materiality by ensuring that only disclosures with clear relevance and information value for users are included. To support this, the wording of ESRS 1.31 should be adjusted to allow for greater flexibility and practicability in applying the materiality concept. This promotes more focused and meaningful reporting while reducing unnecessary disclosure burden.

We propose to amend ESRS 1, Chapter 3 Double materiality as the basis for sustainability disclosures , by adding the following wording:

For the purposes of this Chapter, the information to be disclosed shall be considered as material if its omission or misstatement could influence the decision-making or the judgement of the users of this information. If the information is unlikely to influence their decisions, it might not be deemed material and might be omitted.

## Proposal 3: Prioritisation of Scope 1 and 2 reporting; limitation of Scope 3 reporting for insurers to areas where steering potential exists

When determining priorities for greenhouse gas (GHG) reporting, it is important to differentiate between sectors of the real economy and sectors of the financial industry. Unlike the real economy, the financial industry-particularly through underwriting-has limited potential to influence Scope 3 emissions and to impact behaviour of policy holders. Data availability is often restricted across most lines of business, and there is little to no ability to steer these emissions. Furthermore, the reported data lacks comparability and fails to offer meaningful insights or added value for decision-making.

For insured and financed emissions, the reporting requirement should be limited to categories where an identifiable potential for influence exists, rather than those primarily relevant for monitoring. We propose modifying the wording of ESRS E16 to emphasize the focus on steering potential in the context of GHG emissions reporting. Whether an "identifiable potential for influence" exists should be determined at the discretion of the undertaking.

Scope 3 data should only be reported when it is material and controllable. In this context, it is essential to prioritize high-quality, verifiable data that can directly contribute to internal management, steering, and target-setting processes. Striving for complete data coverage based on low-quality estimates is neither practical nor meaningful. Therefore, we recommend amending ESRS 1.69 to allow companies to omit estimates when such data is not robust enough to support meaningful steering. In such cases, refraining from reporting is preferable to reporting unreliable data whose significance cannot be sufficiently assured.

## Proposal 4: Financial consolidation scope should not be equated with the insurer's own operation activities

The current wording of ESRS 1.62-1.63 does not distinguish between the financial consolidation scope and the undertaking's own operational activities. This lack of clarity may lead to the incorrect assumption that all fully consolidated entities form part of the undertaking's own operations-creating interpretation issues, especially in the case of leased or sub-leased assets that are not under direct operational control. To address this, ESRS 1.62 and 1.63 should be revised to explicitly state that the financial consolidation scope does not automatically define the boundary of operational activities, and to include specific guidance on how to treat leased assets within the sustainability reporting context.

In addition, the current 'layer' concept embedded in ESRS E1-6, which combines financial and operational control to define reporting boundaries, is not aligned with the GHG Protocol and is inappropriate for the insurance industry. Due to the special features of the business model and prudential requirements, insurers are often forced to act as majority investors, which leads to the existence of financial control without being able to exercise operational control over related sustainability matters. This misalignment creates significant challenges for interoperability and comparability across reporting frameworks. ESRS E1-6 should be revised to ensure alignment with the GHG Protocol by allowing recognized consolidation approaches such as financial control, operational control, or the equity share method.

## Proposal 5: Establishing investors as key stakeholders in sustainability reporting

Institutional investors play a key role in financing the sustainable transformation and are the primary users of sustainability reporting data. They require this data firstly to make prudent investment decisions, considering sustainability risks and the impacts of the companies they invest in. Secondly, they use the data to meet their own legal and regulatory sustainability requirements.

For this reason, the GDV, in collaboration with the associations of Germany's private and public banks (BdB and VÖB), has jointly developed an ESG data catalogue for large corporates (Link).The ESG data catalogue has developed as a market standard for banks and insurers and can serve as an example of a reduction in mandatory data points. This catalogue specifies the core data requirements of banks and insurers concerning large companies subject to CSRD reporting. It includes 56 Questions with around 100 key data points on sustainability, covering environmental impact, social responsibility, and governance. The data catalogue is aligned with CSRD, ESRS, EU Taxonomy, EU Disclosure Regulation, and BaFin/EBA guidelines but contains significantly fewer mandatory data points than required by the ESRS.

## Proposal 6: Streamline Narrative Reporting and Prioritize Quantitative Disclosures

To enhance clarity, objectivity, and comparability in sustainability reporting, the focus should be placed on quantitative data points. Narrative elements-particularly repetitive or overly detailed explanations of policies, actions, and targets-should be reduced or consolidated.

## Proposal 7: Deletion of direct references to external (voluntary) standards, such as PCAF, which are not formally part of the ESRS and were not subject to consultation by the European Commission

We suggest removing direct references to external voluntary standards, such as PCAF, not formally part of the ESRS and were not subject to public consultation by the European Commission. The aim is to maintain alignment with the formal legal framework and avoid potential misunderstandings regarding the mandatory nature of such standards. We propose amending ESRS E1-6, AR 46 (b) to delete the reference to the PCAF Standard.
