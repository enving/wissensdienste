# Taxonomieverordnung: GDV fordert weitere Anpassungen

# Kurzfassung

Der Verband hat am 26. März 2025 seine Stellungnahme zu den Anpassungen im Taxonomiereporting abgegeben. Darin begrüßt der Verband die Vorschläge, erwartet jedoch noch weitere Änderungen im laufenden Review.

## Introduction

GDV appreciates the possibility to give feedback on the EU-Commission's (EC) proposals  to  amend  the  Taxonomy  regulation  (i.e.,  Delegated  Regulations 2021/2178, 2021/2139 and 2023/2486). German (re)insurers share the EU-Commission's goals for a climate neutral continent as well as a green transition and we support the efforts made in this respect. Nevertheless, in the last years the reporting burden for (re)insurers has increased heavily.

We strongly believe that a well-balanced and effective sustainable finance framework is key to accelerating the transition to a sustainable economy. Therefore, we have consistently advocated for a targeted reduction of bureaucratic burdens to enable sustainable and meaningful reporting. Further improvements are expected through the ongoing revision of the delegated act in accordance with Article 8 of the taxonomy. We also see great benefit in considering the proposals of the Platform for Sustainable Finance of February 2025.

## Remarks in detail

## 1. Introduction of a 10 % de minimis threshold

Reporting companies may apply a 10 % de minimis threshold (for underwriting premiums and assets) if the premiums and/or the assets represent not more than 10 % of the denominator of the relevant KPI. They may be considered not material and must not be assessed on compliance with the Taxonomy criteria. The extent of not assessed premiums and/or assets must be reported separately.

We fully support this EC's omnibus proposal of a de minimis threshold for the investment KPI. Based on our understanding, auditors have strongly urged companies to collect extensive Taxonomy data even if this would only marginally increase the Taxonomy alignment (e.g., by less than 1 %). Assessing Taxonomy criteria for assets that make no measurable contribution to the reported figures is both burdensome and not meaningful. Furthermore, it should be clarified, if these assets (up to 10 %) should be included in the denominator or not and that the auditor must not request estimated data.

For the underwriting KPI, the benefits are less clear. Once the calculation of the underwriting KPI is integrated into existing internal processes, we do not anticipate a significant simplification. However, for (re)insurers developing new lines of business or in case of small entities, having the option not to implement the calculation in the systems might be valuable. Therefore, we welcome the flexibility to calculate or not to calculate this KPI as needed.

## 2. Simplification of the templates

We fully support the proposed simplification. The adjustments to both the underwriting KPI and the investment KPI (now Green Asset Ratio KPI) will significantly streamline the templates. Nevertheless, we believe, that there is still room for further simplification. In particular, we doubt whether the breakdown of covered assets provides meaningful additional insight from an investor's perspective.

Generally, we recommend aligning the wording of the template with section 1 of annex  IX,  e.g.  using  'total  investments'  or  'total  assets'  for  the  avoidance  of doubts.

Furthermore, a general review of all templates regarding form and logic should be done, e.g. such as pro-rata approach concerning reinsurance.

## 3. The consolidated KPI (between Investment KPI and Underwriting KPI)

This element is not included in EC's omnibus proposal. The consolidated KPI is based on two completely different KPIs and in our view adds no significant informational value. Therefore, we strongly recommend adapting the EU Taxonomy to clarify that aggregating the two insurance KPIs (i.e. consolidated KPI) is not necessary and can be omitted. Additionally, guidance on consolidated KPI should be removed from Q&amp;As. This holds true for any other consolidated KPI of a group of different (financial) companies (e.g. insurers, banks, asset manager).
