# GDV-Stellungnahme zur EU-Konsultation zur ergänzenden Altersvorsorge

# Kurzfassung

Angesichts des demografischen Wandels ist es geboten, die Rentenlücke durch mehr Kapitaldeckung zu verringern. Versicherungsprodukte eignen sich für langfristige Investitionen, bieten Sicherheit und stabile Leistungen. Sie können zugleich Kapital in die Realwirtschaft lenken. Der GDV plädiert für klare und effiziente Rahmenbedingungen. (mehr)

## Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

1. Do you consider that the pension tracking system in your Member State functions well?
- a. Yes
- b. No, it should be extended/improved
- c. No, my country doesn't have a tracking system
- d. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. In case you are not satisfied, please indicate which features should be improved or added.

## Response:

Germany has recently launched the Digitale Rentenübersicht (digital pension overview) as a central tracking system that allows individuals to view their entitlements from the statutory, occupational, and private pension systems. The Digital Pension Overview portal went into full operation on 1 January 2025. All pension institutions that are obliged to transmit status notifications had to connect. All life insurers and IORPs are connected. The challenge remains to increase the number of registered users.

a) access to the system and the information provided is simple and secure: High usability and low access barriers are crucial for broad uptake. The system must be intuitive and digital.

## 3.  Auto -enrolment

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

7. What are in your views the key features for an auto -enrolment mechanism to be successful? (please rank the options according to their importance)
- a. Provision of auto -enrolment administration facilities by the State
- b. Starting with low contribution rates for participants with their gradual escalation over time
- c. Duration and recurrence of opt -out windows and options for re -enrolment
- d. State  incentives (e.g.  tax  or  subsidies),  with  calibration  based  on  income categories
- e. Preservation of statutory pension benefits and sustainability
- f. Full or partial early withdrawal of pension benefits (subject to penalty, where relevant)
- g. Involvement of social partners in its design.

## h. Other (please specify)

Please elaborate your answer.

## Response:

First of all, GDV would like to make the following general comments:

We support voluntary, opt-out-based automatic registration of workers with flexible implementation at the national level: Any standard product should be chosen by employers in cooperation with providers and not centrally prescribed. A standard product prescribed by the state would hinder competition  and  innovation. Providers must compete openly with each other and ensure product diversity.

- d) State incentives (e.g. tax or subsidies), with calibration based on income categories: Financial incentives are crucial to increase acceptance, especially for low- and middle-income earners.
- c)  Duration  and  recurrence  of  opt-out  windows and  options for  re-enrolment: To uphold voluntariness, the system must allow clear, periodic optout and re-entry options. GDV advocated for a non-mandatory opt-out approach that preserves individual choice.
- a) Provision of auto-enrolment administration facilities by the State: GDV opposes a centralised or a mandatory standard product. The default product should be agreed between a concrete employer and provider. Implementation should be open to insurers and existing providers.
- g) Involvement of social partners in its design: The social partners can be involved in the design, but this should not be a mandatory requirement. The type of involvement is important. Employees should be appropriately involved in the introduction of auto -enrolment, in particular through agreements at company level. Collective bargaining should not be a prerequisite. f)  Full  or  partial  early  withdrawal  of  pension  benefits  (subject  to  penalty, where relevant): Not a central feature, since the focus is on long-term retirement income, not liquidity.
- e) Preservation of statutory pension benefits and sustainability: The sus-
6. tainability of pensions in the sense of a lifelong pension phase is important. A central aspect is that there are no disadvantages for the state pension associated with the occupational pension.
- b) Starting with low contribution rates for participants with their gradual escalation over time: That is not decisive. What makes sense is to measure the contribution in relation to income. Saving significant amounts even as a beginner in the profession is valuable because of the compounding effect.

8. In your opinion, what should be the features that the default pension plan(s) should have to be successful? (please rank the options according to their importance)
- a. Life -cycle asset allocation (more prudent as the retirement date approaches)
- b. Option to shift pension plan and risk profile at a later stage (in addition to opt out)
- c. Minimum contribution, with the option to increase it at later stage
- d. Capital guarantee, despite expected lower return compared to solutions without that guarantee
- e. Sufficient scope of target population, to ensure cost effectiveness and investment diversification capability of the default fund(s)
- f. Other
- g. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer.

## Response:

In general, the GDV does not support a standardised default product. Each provider should be able to agree with the employer of the default pension plan.

The default option should not be decided at EU level. The characteristics of the pension schemes vary greatly between Member States. For example, in Germany DB schemes are dominant, therefore, the guarantees are an important feature and a) and b) are not relevant. In other Member States where DCs are dominant, other features might be more important.

f) Other: By far the most important requirement is a lifelong payment of the benefits. Open competition, no state fund, and product diversity are essential. Default product features must not crowd out market-driven innovation or create barriers for providers.

d) Capital guarantee (e.g. partial), despite expected lower return compared to solutions without that guarantee is essential for many savers that are not able or willing to take the full market risk are important for participation of savers.

e) Sufficient scope of target population, to ensure cost effectiveness and investment  diversification  capability  of  the  default  fund(s):  GDV  prefers competition between multiple providers rather than one centralised solution /centralized default fund. In general, a wide scope of target population is important for scale effects.

Minimal contribution with an option to increase is important to achieve scale effects and avoid marginal contribution.

9. In your opinion, who should have the responsibility to establish the default pension plan that eligible participants should enrol in?
- a. The legislator
- b. The social partners, where applicable
- c. The employer
- d. Other
- e. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer.

## Response:

The employer should have the primary responsibility to establish the default pension plan in cooperation with a suitable provider. This approach ensures flexibility, broad applicability, and low administrative burden. It also fits with the basic idea of company pension schemes, which are a benefit provided by the employer to their employees and can be shaped by the employer regarding the amount of payment and the specific pension plan.

10. In your opinion, what measures shall be adopted to ensure equal opportunities for self -employed and employees not covered by auto -enrolment?
- a. Granting of equivalent tax incentives or other subsidies to participate in private pension plans
- b. Granting of equivalent tax incentives or other subsidies to participate in in general default occupational pension plans only
- c. Other
- d. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer.

## Response:

The  self-employed  group  naturally  differs  from  employees  in  many  respects.  It  is  therefore  unclear  why  self-employed  individuals  should  be treated equally to employees in this context with regard to tax incentives, etc.

## 4.  Review of the PEPP Regulation

## Basic PEPP

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

12. In your view, does the current structure of the Basic PEPP allow for wide uptake by savers across the European Union, helping to ensure adequate income in retirement while also contributing meaningfully to the objectives of the Savings and investments union?

- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. What changes, if any, would be necessary to  enhance the attractiveness of the Basic PEPP for both providers and savers?

## Response:

The current structure of both Basic and 'regular' PEPPs has not led to wide uptake in general and no uptake in Germany. In our view, the low take-up is due to an overly complex and overly prescriptive regulatory framework. Key shortcomings include in particular:

- Mandatory sub-accounts across Member States, which creates unnecessary administrative burden and complexity for providers.
- Very ambitious risk mitigation techniques, that require products to be very safe and also have a high performance.
- Restrictive cost cap (1% of invested capital for the Basic PEPP), which prevents providers from offering viable, sustainable products.
- PEPP-specific information documents, instead of allowing consistency with existing PRIIPs KIDs or IDD documentation, increasing bureaucracy and duplication.

To ensure contributing meaningfully to the objectives of the SIU further aspects needs to be regarded:

- A cost caps prevents potential PEPP-providers from investing into alternative assets since they are not as cheap as other assets classes. Investments into private debt, infrastructure, venture capital or private equity is not to be expected under a (ambitious) cost-cap.
- For the goals of the SIU long term and illiquid investments are crucial. A low cost switching service (as required under current PEPP regulation)  forces however providers to invest in liquid assets. Neither the customers nor the SIU and the European economy can then benefit from these alternative assets.

PEPP as a label for national products is a viable alternative. An advantage of labels is that they are efficiently regulated and can be adapted to national specificities of pension products.

13. Do you consider that the Basic PEPP should necessarily be designed with a built -in lifecycle investment strategy, as a standard feature of the product?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. Please consider whether other risk mitigation  techniques  should  also  be  considered  as  a  standard  feature  of  the Basic PEPP and why.

## Response:

The Basic PEPP should not be required to follow a built-in lifecycle investment strategy as a standard feature. Instead, the choice of risk mitigation techniques should be left to the discretion of the providers, allowing providers to adapt the product to different saver profiles, national markets, and product designs. Furthermore, life cycling does not work for products that enjoy a balanced risk reward profile throughout its lifetime such as collective products.

14. Do you consider that the Basic PEPP should be designed in a way that it can be offered also on an execution -only basis (i.e. without requiring investment advice)?
- a. Yes
- b. No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, what additional design features could support or facilitate the distribution of the Basic PEPP on an execution -only basis? Additionally, do you consider that there would be value in linking such distribution to a condition that contributions remain within the nationally applicable tax -deductible limits?

## Response:

We believe that the same regulatory rules should apply to the Basic PEPP as to comparable national pension products. Allowing execution-only distribution for the Basic PEPP while subjecting national products to stricter requirements would contradict the level playing field approach.

Therefore, we do not consider it appropriate to create EU-specific rules to allow execution-only distribution for Basic PEPP. To ensure fair competitive conditions across the EU, the regulatory frame for PEPP should be aligned with national laws.

15. Do you consider it is useful to maintain the availability of alternative investment options, in addition to the Basic PEPP?
- a. Yes
- b. No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, should such options be defined and if yes, what should be such additional investment options and what should their purpose be (e.g., making the PEPP more aligned with an employer matching  scheme,  offering  a  broader  PEPP  investment  portfolio,  etc.), while ensuring they remain consistent with the PEPP's objectives?

## Response:

It is essential to maintain the availability of alternative investment options in addition to the Basic PEPP. A functioning PEPP market requires product diversity to address the heterogeneity of saver preferences, life situations, and national pension frameworks. Relying solely on the Basic PEPP as a default solution would be too narrow and could lead to low uptake.

## Sub-accounts

16. In your view, does the sub -account structure align effectively with the specificities inherent in a cross -border product, including how Member States grant tax or other relevant incentives for personal pension products?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If no, what alternative structure would better serve the objectives of the PEPP?

## Response:

First  of  all,  in  the  case  that  PEPP  is  targeting  a  broad marked, the vast majority of savers will save and receive their pensions in the same Member State. Designing the entire structure around the rare case of mobile citizens ignores the reality of pension savers and is economically not sensible. Subaccounts cause costs due to the required adjustments to different national legal, tax, social and pensions systems.

Instead,  accumulated  capital  should  be  transferrable  to  another  PEPPprovider in another member state, when the customer changes its place of residence.

17. Do you consider the requirement for PEPP providers to offer sub -accounts for at least two Member States is necessary to foster cross -border provision of PEPPs?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. In addition, should the Regulation ensure that savers have access to a PEPP from any PEPP provider, regardless of their Member State of residence and without requiring a sub -account to be available in that Member State?

## Response:

The mandatory provision of several sub-accounts forces providers to implement and maintain multiple national structures within one product, each aligned with different tax and legal rules - creating high administrative burden and legal uncertainty without real added value.

Furthermore, a product with few sub-accounts is not interesting for many mobile EU citizens since it is often not predictable at the time of contract signing in which other member states their professional or family situation will lead (and thus in which member states the sub accounts are required).

Thus, PEPP should also function effectively as a product designed for use within a single Member State.

In general, PEPP should therefore aim to find a more pragmatic solution to allow the mobility of savers between Member States, such as the transfer of accumulated capital to a new provider. Compartments could be set up on a voluntary basis.

## Fee cap

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

18. Do you consider that the Basic PEPP should continue to be subject to a 1 % fee cap?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If no, what alternative measures would you propose to keep the cost of the Basic PEPP at affordable levels?

## Response:

The current cost cap of 1% of capital accumulated for the basic PEPP is very restrictive. This cost cap hinders good uptake and prevent economies of scale, since it does not consider excessive detailed requirements on the PEPP, which result in high costs for providers.

Consumers  should  be  able  to  trust  that  products  offer  them  value  for money. Since the introduction of the PEPP, the consumer protection regulation has been strengthened further. The introduction of a benchmark with dedicated  clusters  to  PEPP  products  will  reinforce  the  focus  on  costeffectiveness and allow supervisory authorities to intervene if needed. For these reasons, a cost cap is not necessary.

Furthermore, the cost cap contradicts the goal of SIU to foster investments into the European economy. A low cost cap forces providers to strictly costoptimise their products, requiring them to base products on low cost ETF of large corporations - mostly seated outside of EU. Alternative assets, which play a central role for the goals of SIU, are illiquid, cannot be traded as easily,  and  are  not  comparably  cheap  like  ETFs.  A  low  cost  cap  would there-fore exclude customers from this attractive asset class and prevent investments in projects that are important for the SIU.

Notwithstanding this, as PEPPs are intended to be held until retirement, it would be more appropriate to change the current perspective on costs in the PEPP Regulation and consider average annual costs during the accumulation phase through Reduction in Yield instead. This would ensure fair treatment of products with higher set-up costs and lower long-term costs, without incurring more costs over the entire term of the contract.

19. If the fee cap for the Basic PEPP were to be maintained, do you think certain cost components (e.g. taxes, specific distribution costs) should be excluded from the cap, or that other adjustments to the cap should be considered?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, please specify which types of costs you believe should be excluded or what adjustments should be considered, and explain why:

## Response:

The current 1% fee cap for the Basic PEPP is not suitable, as it fails to reflect  the  cost-intensive  regulatory  requirements.  From our perspective, the concept of value for money is far more appropriate than a fixed cap.

Excluding certain costs from the cost cap is not a good approach. First, because it is misleading for customers when a product legally has a cost cap of 1 % but actually has higher costs for them. Second, because excluding certain cost components creates a high administrative burden for product manufacturers without providing any recognizable benefit for customers. A cost limit that only works when certain costs are artificially excluded should be completely avoided.

## Risk-mitigation techniques

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

20. In your view, do the existing risk -mitigation requirements strike an appropriate balance between ensuring consumer protection and maintaining sufficient flexibility and incentive for PEPP providers to offer the PEPP?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If no, which aspects do you find problematic, and how might they be improved?

## Response:

Although risk mitigation techniques (RMT) are very important, the current provisions should be improved. Appropriate risk mitigation is a key issue in retirement savings. In particular, it can help convince risk-averse savers to invest. Risk tolerance and the ability to bear losses are a central component of the suitability and appropriateness assessment. This is already covered by the IDD (or similar MiFID).

However, the current rules on RMTs are rather contradictory. They require a strict quantitative proof that each PEPP is both safe and high-yielding. Both  objectives  cannot  be  achieved  equally  well.  Moreover,  compliance with the current highly prescriptive Level 2 measures depends more on current capital market conditions than on the products themselves. This means that the same product will sometimes comply and sometimes not. The requirements should therefore be more principle-based and less prescriptive.

There are also different types of RMTs. From the savers' perspective, guarantees are a particularly easy form of RMT to understand. Products with terminal (non-permanent) guarantees can generate significant returns and allow  investment  in  the  real  economy  while  maintaining  an  appropriate risk/return profile for the saver. They should therefore continue to be explicitly permitted as RMTs.

Not all savers have a long-term investment horizon up to retirement age. Therefore, the claim that risk would decrease with time does not apply in general. The current option of postponing the start of the pension by 3 years in the event of market value losses exposes savers to an even great-er risk. The minimum requirement for a PEPP should therefore be a gradual reduction in capital market risk as the retirement age approaches.

Before considering extending PEPP to the workplace context, it should first establish itself as an effectively regulated private pension product. There are many obstacles to extending it to occupational pensions, as this would require alignment with national social and labour law frameworks. Occupational pensions are very traditional and closely linked to national statutory pension systems. Additionally, such an extension would cause systemic disruptions, as occupational pensions are regulated differently from private pensions.

## Investment rules and diversification

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following question:

24. Do you consider the investment rules in the PEPP Regulation appropriate to support the achievement of adequate long -term returns?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer.

## Response:

There should be no PEPP-specific investment rules. PEPP should follow the sectoral rules. Divergent rules would lead to an unlevel playing field. Germany has issued additional rules for insurers as PEPP providers on the separate management of assets, which prohibit actuarial risk sharing collectively with other IBIPs during the accumulation phase. This is a further, specifically national obstacle for providers.

## Level playing field across personal pension providers and rules on distribution

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

25. Do you consider that PEPP's limited uptake is due to the existence of competing personal pension products across the Member States?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, what key features do you think give existing national products a competitive advantage over the PEPP? Please provide examples. Should the European Commission adjust the PEPP to allow it to be more competitive with national products? If so, what kind of adjustments should be considered and how could the framework be improved?

## Response:

No. The limited uptake of the PEPP is not primarily due to the existence of competing national personal pension products, but rather to regulatory and design flaws within the PEPP framework itself. Distributors and providers do not systematically avoid PEPP-like products in favour of more lucrative alternatives. Provider reluctance stems from regulation complexity not from an inherent preference for high-margin products.

In fact, there are existing long-term products on the insurance market today that have long-term cost structures comparable to the PEPP.

## Investment rules and diversification

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

34. Do you consider that a diversified portfolio of assets, including also investments in unlisted securities or alternative assets classes (with proper management and adequate risk safeguards) could enhance long -term returns for scheme members and beneficiaries?
- a. Yes
- b. No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. Please justify your answer based on data, if available. Furthermore, please elaborate what are in your view the risks and benefits associated with a share of IORPs assets being allocated to alternative assets, and which alternative asset classes would be more suitable and how would hereto related risks be best managed.

## Response:

In  principle,  a  diversified  portfolio  that  includes  alternative  asset  classes and unlisted securities can enhance long-term returns, provided that proper risk management and adequate safeguards are in place. However, in this context, we would like to point out the following. The requirements for the Prudent Person Principle (PPP) for insurers under Solvency II (Article 132 of the Solvency II- Directive) and IORPs under Solvency I (Article 19 IORP II-Directive) are largely the same. For fundamental reasons and for the sake of maintaining a level playing field, we do not consider it justified to unilaterally soften the PPP-requirements for IORPs, whereas the rules for Solvency II insurers, which are subject to significantly higher qualitative risk management requirements under Pillar 1 of Solvency II in comparison to IORPs, remain unchanged.

In Germany, the BaFin places the requirement in Article 19d IORP II-Directive 'to keep investments in assets which are not admitted to trading on regulated markets to prudent levels' in the context of the company's individual situation. The level that is considered prudent is determined in particular by the IORP's own risk-bearing capacity. Apart from that, the German market is dominated by defined benefit (DB) schemes and the limited application of defined contribution (DC) models. The possibility of DC plans without any employer liability for a guaranteed benefit exists only on the basis of corresponding agreements between the collective bargaining parties ("social partner model').

35. Are there in your knowledge any national quantitative or other type of investment rules imposing overly restrictive limits on investments in alternative assets?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, what is the rationale for such limits and should Member States continue to be allowed to impose such limits, despite the reliance on a risk -based supervisory approach? If investment limitation rules continue to be allowed under the IORP Directive, do you consider it important to place limits on overly restrictive national rules in certain asset classes, including unlisted assets? Please also indicate which types of restrictions you consider most problematic and how they could be addressed without undermining appropriate risk control.

## Response:

As already said in the answer to question 34 BaFin places the requirement in Article 19d IORP II-Directive 'to keep investments in assets which are not admitted to trading on regulated markets to prudent levels' in the context of the company's individual situation. The level that is considered prudent is determined in particular by the IORP's own risk-bearing capacity. We consider this interpretation as sensible with a view to qualitative risk management. Furthermore, the German supervisory authority is flexible in adjusting the national investment rules (Anlageverordnung) to market conditions and has recently extended the options for investing in alternative investments, particularly infrastructure.

- a. Yes
- b.  No

## Response:

Expertise is generally linked to the size of the portfolios managed by pension funds. As the pension funds we represent generally belong to insurance groups, sufficient expertise also for unlisted assets is regularly ensured.  Smaller  IORPs  may  not  necessarily  lack  expertise.  They  can achieve expertise by investing in e. g. unlisted asset classes via collective investment vehicles under the current framework.

37. Do  you  consider  that  the  current  provisions  on  risk  management  in  the IORP II Directive and the intervention capacity of supervisory authorities could be further enhanced to strengthen trust in institutions under the scope of the Directive?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, please specify in what ways these aspects could be improved. In particular, do you consider that the existing framework provides adequate transparency on IORPs' use of derivatives, as well as the use of investment vehicles and private credit transactions? If no, please elaborate how any existing gaps should be addressed.

## Response:

In our view, the risk management provisions are sufficiently specific in the IORP II Directive. In particular, the main risk management areas are addressed. The Directive explicitly includes in Art. 25 (2) lit c investment, in particular derivatives, securitisations and similar commitments. Particularly noteworthy are the investment rules in Art. 19, which ensure, among other things, that derivative instruments shall only be possible insofar as such instruments contribute to a reduction in investment risks or facilitate efficient portfolio management. According to Art. 46 lit g) and h) investment rules and investment management are part of the prudential supervision.

Additional regulation results in effort and additional costs. It would be a burden on companies and would tend to run counter to the goal of making occupational pension provision more widespread.

38. Do you consider that the introduction of an explicit duty of care provision could further strengthen the level of protection of members and beneficiaries?
- a. Yes
- b.  No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If such a duty were to be made explicit in the Directive, what elements should it cover?

## Response:

Already under current law, IORPs are under an obligation to act in good faith towards their members and beneficiaries and the employer. This obligation arises from the contractual relationship. Apart from this, the mutual duties and responsibilities of the IORP, its members and beneficiaries, as well as the employer, are determined by the contract and the applicable law. Any further requirement to act in accordance with the best interests of members and beneficiaries  would  need  to  be  principle-based  and  sufficiently abstract in order to match all conceivable providers, configurations and situations. Any such requirement would also need to allow for arrangements, where the IORP has contractual obligations towards not only the members and beneficiaries, but also the employer. It would furthermore have to consider that the individual interests of one particular beneficiary and the collective interests of the community of beneficiaries are not always identical. A balancing of interests would have to remain possible. Furthermore, if the introduction of a duty of care would come with additional documentation and reporting requirements to allow NCAs its supervision.

This would again result in additional administrative burden and additional cost. This would tend to hinder the further spread of occupational pension provision.

## Scale

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

40. Do you consider that the scale of many IORPs may affect their overall investment capacity, for example by reducing their ability to build a diversified portfolio, hindering the performance of the schemes due to cost inefficiencies, or by creating other inefficiencies?
- a. Yes

Please elaborate your answer. Do you believe that national competent authorities should have an explicit mandate to oversee and, where appropriate, intervene in order to help ensure that supplementary pension schemes deliver adequate investment returns for members and beneficiaries? If yes, what tools or powers should supervisors be equipped with to address situations where schemes systematically fail to deliver good outcomes?

## Response:

The NCAs such as BaFin in Germany, are adequately equipped under the current IORP II Directive to oversee that assets are invested in the best long-term interests of members and beneficiaries. Additional regulation at the EU level is not necessary. Article 19 of the IORP II Directive already requires investments to be made in accordance with the prudent person principle, focusing on security, quality, liquidity, and profitability in the long term. The Own Risk Assessment (ORA) and other supervisory tools provide sufficient transparency and control.

Finally, German IORPs are mostly DB-based; investment decisions are often predefined by the pension promise and are not discretionary.

We strongly oppose any extension of supervisory powers that would allow NCAs to intervene directly in investment decisions with the aim of securing a particular level of 'adequate investment returns'. Investment returns can never  be  guaranteed  and  are  subject  to  market  fluctuations.  Moreover, such intervention would raise serious governance concerns, undermining the accountability and responsibility of those charged with managing investments, and potentially creating agency risks. Within the Prudent Person Principle NCAs already have sufficient discretionary powers to take regulatory  action  via  the  investment  principles  of security,  quality,  liquidity  and profitability. That is why supervisory oversight should remain focused on prudential standards and solvency, not on assessing or steering specific return levels.

## b.  No

- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If yes, are you aware of any best practices which can facilitate the build -up of scale in the IORPs sector (e.g. asset pooling, fiduciary management,  outsourced  chief investment officer, multi -employer schemes, master trust arrangements) In particular, are you aware of any obstacles or difficulties (including but not limited to cross -border issues) preventing scale -up or any of the above -mentioned practices? Please indicate if and how the review of the IORP II Directive can foster the take up of such practices or otherwise contribute to the potential scale -up of workplace pension schemes?

## Response:

IORPs represented by the GDV are operated via life insurers and are part of larger corporate groups. The investment capacities of these IORPs are sufficient to diversify their portfolios. Maybe there are small IORPs in the German market whose investment capacities are not sufficient to build a diversified portfolio themselves. However, these smaller IORPs can build a diversified portfolio and achieve access to e.g. unlisted asset classes by investing via collective investment vehicles. That is why we do not consider that  the  scale  of  IORPs  necessarily  limits  their  investment  capacity  or causes structural inefficiencies. While certain economies of scale can improve cost-efficiency and diversification options, the size of an IORP alone is not a meaningful indicator of investment quality or scheme performance.

## Minimum standards

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

45. In your view, does the existing framework ensure a level playing field for all providers under the scope of the Directive across the European Union?
- a. Yes
- b. No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If no, what are the main sources of imbalance or fragmentation, and how could the review of the IORP II Directive be improved to support regulatory and supervisory consistency across providers and Member States?

## Response:

The IORP II Directive follows a minimum harmonisation approach, which the GDV supports. The current framework establishes adequate minimum standards and a proportionate supervisory regime, which allows NCAs to apply the rules in line with the nature, scale, and complexity of IORPs. This approach is suitable given the heterogeneity of pension systems, especially differences  in  labour,  tax,  and  social  law.  Trying  to  impose  fully  uniform rules would not improve fairness, but rather distort functioning national systems. Additional regulation would hinder the further spread of occupational pension provision.

There is no empirical evidence of IORPs exploiting differences in national regulation to engage in regulatory arbitrage.

Proportionality is a core principle of the IORP II Directive. Fairness and level playing field are better achieved by allowing NCAs to tailor supervision to local risks and safeguards (e.g., existence of pension protection schemes), rather than imposing uniform additional minimum standards.

## Transparency, information and pension tracking systems

Stakeholders' views are sought on the following:

48. In your view, are the current rules in the IORP II Directive sufficient to ensure that all members and beneficiaries receive clear and effective information (e.g. on cost disclosure, performance, risk indicators and benefit projections)?
- a. Yes
- b. No
- c. No opinion

Please elaborate your answer. If no, which aspects of the information requirements are most lacking, and how could the regulatory framework be improved?

## Response:

The current rules in the IORP II Directive are sufficient to ensure that members and beneficiaries receive clear and effective information, including on costs, performance, risks, and benefit projections. They are now part of national supervisory law.

Given the different specificities across member states and IORPs, the Directive's rules for information and transparency must continue to be principle-based, with minimum harmonisation provisions, and not overly detailed to ensure flexibility. It is also important to emphasise that the relevance of information, for example on costs and performance, varies depending on the  pension  scheme  type  (DB  or  DC).  Cost  disclosures  are  meaningful where members bear investment risk, but should not be overstated in DB schemes or in the context of 'Transformationsgeschäft', where employees have no influence over the product or investment decisions and no cost impact.

We believe that the national characteristics can best be taken into account by the NCA. For example an already existing reasonable reporting system to the NCA should not be counteracted by European requirements with an unusual and not purposeful level of detail or completely different logic. Generalised requirements would inevitably lead to increased effort without any discernible gain in transparency. This would cause confusion and unnecessary costs for members and beneficiaries. This might contradict EIOPA's objectives regarding efficiency, affordability and good value for money.

The German NCAs assessment of costs and charges in 2022 / 2023 has shown that there is no cost problem in German occupational pension sector. The total cost level of German pension funds is appropriate at 0.79 % of investments. The German NCA does not see any benefit in installing a comprehensive regular cost reporting system along the lines of the EIOPA 2021  option.  It  does  not  expect  that  a  comprehensive  reporting  system would lead to noticeable savings.
